## The Forwarding Loop (continued)

### Acting on a step

| `kind` | What you do |
|--------|-------------|
| `print` | When the directive carries `narration`, say it first. Do exactly what `directive.message` says: it is authoritative. When it names a command to run and then `next` (an `intent-create` that starts the work, for one), run both. A `print` from a refused `report` is your next step: when it says their question is still open, keep it open and never answer it for them; when it names the choice they made, record that choice. |
| `error` | Print `directive.message` verbatim and STOP. Do not recover, retry, or smooth it over: the message is the user-facing error. When it names no step for the person, add one line: /aidlc --doctor shows what to fix. |
| `done` | When `directive.workflow_continues === true`, the step is recorded and the workflow goes on: run bare `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next` at once and act on what it returns, without a completion summary and without saying the work is finished. If the person's reply that led to this step also asked to stop the workflow there for now (not to pause on one decision inside the work), run `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate park --person-asked` instead and act on its `parked` directive. Otherwise the workflow (or single-stage run) is complete: present the completion summary and STOP the loop. |
| `parked` | The workflow was parked at a clean inter-stage boundary (`directive.stage`) for a later session. Say its `narration`, then STOP the loop. A `park` advances no stage and marks nothing complete; a `report` that answers `parked` recorded the person's answer first, because the same reply asked to stop the workflow there for now. |
| `notice` | Print `directive.message` verbatim and STOP; this is the deterministic, terminal Team Construction board (grid, observed claims, merge readiness, claimable and blocked Units), so do not summarize it or run `next`/`report`. |
| `load-steering` | The stage's rules arrive in parts, ahead of the step they belong to (rare). Apply the rules each part carries and keep them as the active stage's rules for the rest of the run; the first part of a piece of work may also carry how you work for the whole run: adopt it, once. Do not print a progress message or mention the parts to the person, and do not put a sentence of your own where the progress message would have gone: loading rules is not an event in the person's project, so no wording of it, however plain, belongs in the transcript; a warning or notice a part carries is said once, from the stage step it leads to. Run the command the part names (copy it as given) and act on what comes back, until the stage step arrives; never report a part. A part AI-DLC no longer recognises is answered with the current step: follow it. |
| `run-stage` | **A Construction step (a Unit or batch checkpoint, a team gate, a Construction policy) is routed by the Construction module first: its **Construction directive routing** says how.** Apply the rules the step carries, or the ones this chat already holds for it, before anything else and without narrating them. Say the step's line for the person first, in the same message as your first reads, and say any warning it carries as written. Then do what the step's reading instruction says: one file at a time, waiting for every result, before the stage file, the inputs, the diary (`directive.memory_path` is engine-created and output-only; follow the Kiro diary write discipline above whenever an observation arises), the stage body, any dispatch or any output; nothing is loaded until you have read it. Then branch by what the step is, below, before running the stage body or writing its outputs: an isolated run, or the ordinary step with its gate (a settled swarm and a wave follow the module the step names). On a dispatched topology, deliver the stage's rules per `stage-protocol.md` § "For subagent stages" step 2 and paste the step's brief lines into every agent brief and include the Kiro diary write discipline verbatim. A missing input, the way the person answers the stage's questions, a plan's status and the summary confirmation each arrive as the step's own instruction when they apply: follow it. |
| `ask` | A question for the person. Show it with its choices as given, in the conversation's language (even one that lists a single piece of work is asked and waited for): never replace it, drop or add a choice, add a recommendation, or run another tool or read state first. Say the step's numbered question as given, its Other line included, per `question-rendering.md`: the final displayed option is Other in that form, and a trailing "or tell me" does not replace it. Then END THE TURN and act only on their reply. A reply that picks one of the choices (its number, its words, or their own words for it) runs that choice's command; act on the step it returns, or run `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next` when it returns none. Anything else is their own words: read what they meant; a continue or reshape answer that needs the piece of work named first gets only the pieces listed, as a numbered list with Other, and the turn ends. Never answer a question through `report`. A guard's question (its ways on): when they are your own work, carry out the first that applies under **Guard-recovery execution** below without showing the question; otherwise show the choices as given and, once one is picked, record it with that choice's command and follow its way on under **Guard-recovery execution** below; a question with no ways on is terminal: show it as written and take no engine action until they answer. When the person's last reply already asked for changes and said what should change, that is their choice of the `request-changes` remedy: follow it with their words instead of presenting the ask. The plan question's three choices keep their labels exactly as given, in any language: the person's pick is matched on them; it records their choice as its instruction says, then `next`. When the person asks on re-entry to redo, jump to a stage, or start fresh (at an approval gate too, where it is that request and not the gate's answer), call `report --result resumed --choice <redo|jump|fresh>` with the choice you read from their words (add `--target <stage slug>` for the stage they named, and `--unit <unit>` or `--every-unit` when they named a Unit or said every Unit), then act on the returned per-choice `print` (it names the exact command or follow-up for that choice). That request is not a question AI-DLC asked, and is the only non-stage `report` round-trip. AI-DLC never asks the person itself: it defers the human turn to you. |
| `invoke-swarm` | Load every module named in `directive.protocol_modules` before acting - including `reviewer` when emitted, plus `construction` and `swarm` - then follow the swarm module's subsection for this harness. The directive kind is the fallback trigger when the hint field is absent. |

**Recorded swarm choice.** Resolve `AIDLC_USE_SWARM` env-first. When it is unset, read the merged `flags.swarm` from `aidlc.settings.local.json`, project `aidlc.settings.json`, and the machine `aidlc.settings.json`; local wins over project, and project wins over machine. `true` behaves as `"1"`, while `false` or an absent value behaves as unset.

When the reply to the question about where new words belong is only `4` or `Other`, your whole message is **"What would you like me to do instead?"**: call no tool and end your turn. When they answer, run `next '<their words>'` with their words unchanged and act on what it returns; never call `report`.

**Guard-recovery execution.** After the human selects an executable remedy, branch on its `interaction`:

- `command`: execute the exact returned `command`, backed by its structured `operation`, in the emitted native or source form. The selection is sufficient to attempt this command; it is not Plan Approval or a review verdict. Follow any remaining `action` through the existing protocol only after success.
- `human-input`: render the action's follow-up and END THE TURN. For Request Changes, when the reply that picked it already says what should change, record the pick as `--details 'request-changes: <what they said>'` and report with that reply, unchanged, as the single-quoted reason without asking again. Otherwise ask **"What should change?"** as a structured question per `question-rendering.md` whose options are concrete changes drawn from the stage artifact, never a bare open-ended prompt, and wait for a separate answer; their next reply is the feedback. Only after the human answers may you use the normal `report` or `next` route with their exact text. Preserve Request Changes feedback unchanged as the report's `--reason`, separate from `--user-input "Request Changes"`; obtain a concrete Scope when the action requires one.
- `external-work`: perform the described `action` through its existing protocol and tools. Selection needs no extra feedback turn, but does not supply missing arguments or prove the work completed.

Never reconstruct a command from prose, invent missing arguments, or execute angle-bracket placeholders. When a selected command invokes the orchestrator (the `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate` route, or `aidlc-orchestrate.ts` directly), process its returned directive through the table above. A tool refusal whose last line is a guard-recovery ask JSON follows the same ask contract; render it instead of retrying the refused command. A hook refusal that ends with `Next:` and a command names your next step: run that command and act on the directive it returns; never retry the refused call. For any other tool failure, say in one plain sentence what did not work and name the choices the ask still offers (or /aidlc --doctor when it offers none), and stop that recovery attempt; do not claim success or invent a recovery directive.

**Parking a workflow.** A long workflow (enterprise scope spans many stages) need not finish in one session. Park when the user wants to stop and continue later: run `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate park --person-asked` to park the workflow cleanly at the current inter-stage boundary; it emits a `parked` directive you act on as above. Only the person parks: never park on your own to hand them a decision, such as a next stage that looks outside the plan, work you just set up, or a check that refused. Carry on with the next stage, or ask your question and wait for their answer in this conversation. Never ask the person whether to continue, pause or take a break, however long the run has been; they can say stop at any time. **Do not park because context *feels* heavy.** You cannot measure your own context window, and a conversation that feels long is routinely under half full — a 32-stage run that felt heavy has been measured at 37% used. Never park for context on your own: Kiro gives you no figure, so the engine reads Kiro's own record of this chat and, with an approval question or a Unit's checkpoint, offers the person once to pause and carry on in a new chat (right after Kiro summarizes the chat, or at 80% or more when Kiro's auto-compaction is off), or, when this chat's first turn on the work already took half the window of the model it still runs on, says once that a new chat on the same model will not help much; their answer decides. Never advance or approve stages you did not actually run just to reach `done`: run them, or ask the person and wait.

### Branching a `run-stage` on its gate

For directives not already handled by Construction routing, `run-stage` folds the approval-gate decision into its `gate` field. The engine has already decided whether this stage gates for every deterministic case — bootstrap initialization stages auto-proceed (`gate: false`), every other EXECUTE stage gates (`gate: true`). One case is **not** deterministic and arrives as the sentinel `gate: "unresolved"`, with a note saying how to settle it. The rest:

- **`gate: false`** — initialization stages run and complete directly with no Q&A. A per-unit directive is different: run its question flow and, only when the step asks for the summary confirmation, that confirmation as the step's note names it, recording the receipt with `--unit "<directive.unit>"`, before writing this unit's artifacts; then re-run `next` as the per-unit branch below requires. No workflow approval or learnings ritual fires on either path.
- **Conditional inapplicability**: if the active stage's own condition check proves it cannot run, do not fabricate artifacts or mark it complete. `report --stage "<directive.stage>" --result skipped --reason '<specific reason>'`, then re-run `next`. When Construction runs unit by unit (`Construction Iteration: unit-major`) and the directive names a unit (`directive.unit`), add `--unit "<directive.unit>"`: the skip covers that unit only. Otherwise omit `--unit`: the skip covers every unit. Skip is main-workflow routing: the explicit stage and nonblank reason are mandatory, and `report --single` cannot use it.
- **`gate: true`** — when the step does not ask for the summary confirmation, run the question flow and generate artifacts directly with no summary checkpoint or receipt, then follow the completion sequence below. When it does, run the stage body through its pre-generation question checkpoint before producing artifacts. Do not treat Q&A as complete until the questions file contains `[Answer]: Looks correct` (the choice the human's reply names) and the checkpoint-specific `aidlc-log.ts answer` command succeeds. If the reply is **Other** with no words of their own, do not write or log it; discuss what the human wants instead, re-present all three visible choices in the same turn, and END THE TURN. Otherwise read their reply, write the choice they made in `[Answer]:`, and record it with `--details "Looks correct"` or `--details 'Request changes: <what they asked to change>'`; the tool refuses when the two disagree. When they asked a question, answer it and END THE TURN; ask one short question only when their intent is genuinely unclear, and never ask them to retype a choice. Render and wait for that numbered question per `question-rendering.md`; only after that separate human turn and receipt may the stage body produce artifacts and enter this ordered completion sequence:
  1. **Reviewer protocol:** Load `{{HARNESS_DIR}}/aidlc-common/protocols/stage-protocol-reviewer.md` when the engine lists `reviewer` in `directive.protocol_modules` (or, as a fallback, when `directive.reviewer` is present), and follow it before stage-completion verification.
  2. Run stage-completion verification (artifacts exist, guardrails respected).
  3. Only when `directive.protocol_modules` lists `learnings`, take the **section 13 learnings step**. It asks the person something only when `{{HARNESS_DIR}}/aidlc-common/protocols/stage-protocol-learnings.md` section 13 (When to run) says so, after a correction they made in this stage; otherwise it asks nothing and you go straight to step 4. When it asks, `{{INVOKE}} engine learnings surface --slug <stage-slug> --correction '<their change>'` (one `--correction` per correction, in single quotes, or a `--correction-file` as the module's step 2 says) prints each question with its exact options (`Yes, for this project` / `Yes, for the team` / `No`); render it as a structured question (per `question-rendering.md`), log it like any structured question (section 3) with `--question-kind learnings-note`, and END YOUR TURN at it exactly as at the gate: the approval gate is a separate, later turn, never rendered in the same message. Never infer their answer. Run the admission conflict-check against `aidlc/spaces/<space>/memory/org.md`, then write what they keep with your file tool to the `selections_file` that `surface` returned and run `{{INVOKE}} engine learnings persist --slug <stage-slug> --selections-json <that selections_file>`. A note they asked you to keep for next time is kept the same way with no question, and you say in one line what you kept and where. Advisory and additive: it never blocks the gate. When the module is absent, skip this step entirely.
  4. Open the gate through the engine: `report --stage "<directive.stage>" --result awaiting-approval`.
  5. Present the approval gate as a structured question with every currently applicable choice. **STOP your turn here — do NOT call any tool until the user explicitly responds with their choice.** If the reply is **Other** with no words of their own, do not report it; discuss what the human wants instead, re-present the original gate with every offered semantic choice plus the final numbered Other, and END THE TURN. Otherwise read their reply and record the choice they made. When they approved and asked for something, record the approval, then do it and say in one line what you changed; when they asked a question, answer it and END THE TURN; ask one short question only when their intent is genuinely unclear, and never ask them to retype a choice. Approval is lifecycle reporting, not question logging: never call `aidlc-log.ts decision` or `aidlc-log.ts answer` for this gate. On approval, `report --stage "<directive.stage>" --result approved --user-input "Approve"` (add `--park` when they also asked to stop for now). **Practices Discovery is the ordered exception:** after its human Approve, run the stage body's `practices-promote` first; only that tool may record the affirmed timestamp and `PRACTICES_AFFIRMED` audit receipt. The engine requires both facts from the current Practices Discovery attempt before it accepts `approved`, so a missing, stale, or failed promotion leaves the gate open and the stage incomplete. When they ask for changes, `report --stage "<directive.stage>" --result rejected --user-input "Request Changes"`: their words are kept as the feedback (add `--reason` only to say more, or with what they asked for when their words were not kept); ask "What should change?" only when they did not say, then run the Keep/Modify/Redo loop (re-running the §12a reviewer step when the revision changed a `produces[]` artifact and the directive carries a reviewer - fresh request, fresh dispatch record, fresh review record), then `report --stage "<directive.stage>" --result revised` before re-presenting. Never call lifecycle verbs on `aidlc-state.ts` directly.

**Per-unit batch waves.** On a wave, give every builder its `entry.unit_memory_path`, and include the Kiro diary write discipline verbatim. Every `entry.unit_memory_path` is engine-created and output-only.

## Routing

Each step names the stage protocol and the modules it runs by. Two protocols no step names are loaded at these moments:

- `{{HARNESS_DIR}}/aidlc-common/protocols/stage-protocol-recovery.md` - load on session resume, or when a change event is detected mid-stage.
- `{{HARNESS_DIR}}/aidlc-common/protocols/stage-protocol-governance.md` - load at phase boundaries.

When the step does not ask for the summary confirmation, generate directly from the stage answers with no consolidated-summary checkpoint or receipt. When `directive.ceremony.sensors === "off"`, `sensors_applicable` is empty and no sensor correction or rerun instructions apply. These switches never waive ordinary artifact verification, required decisions, Plan Approval, or the stage approval gate.

Before running a stage body, read every module named in `directive.protocol_modules`, and the stage file, with your file tool in parts of at most 200 lines (offset and limit), from the first line to the last. Kiro shows only part of a long file, and a read that comes back cut short is not all of it: never act on a module or stage file until its last part has come back. Read every other AI-DLC file you are sent to (an agent file, a knowledge file) the same way. Skip a module already read to its last line earlier in the session.

### New work while an intent is active — offer a second intent

When an intent is already active, words that are not this step's answer come back as AI-DLC's own question (continue the active work, start separate work, or reshape the plan), each choice with its command: ask it as the `ask` row says. Two rules hold whatever you judge: never `report` to back out of a step you never acted on (a back-out report records a gate rejection the person never made), and never park, report, or otherwise touch the active work to make room for new work.

- **On a PLAN-RESHAPE signal, route through the compose verb - never forward the raw text.** A mid-flow freeform `next` with no verb advances the current stage, so a reshape request forwarded verbatim would silently run a stage instead of re-shaping the plan. Your first engine call becomes `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next compose '<their words>'`, and the engine's with-state compose dispatch owns the flow from there - UNLESS the request names the stages to skip or add, or the plan to switch to, in which case your first engine call is `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next --skip <slugs>` or `--add <slugs>`, or `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next --scope <plan>`, instead, which changes the plan at once (see "Named stage changes are done at once" and "A named plan is switched at once" below). When the request adds, splits or drops a Unit, the compose call is `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next compose --unit-plan '<their words>'`. This does not weaken the verbatim rule: it is the same sanctioned pre-forward judgment step as the new-work offer, and everything after the judgment rides the deterministic verb. (The literal `/aidlc compose "<request>"` verb remains the documented reliable path on this harness.)
- You switch between intents any time with `/aidlc intent <name>` (bare `/aidlc intent` lists them) — parallel to `/aidlc space <name>`. Work you are done with but never finished is retired with `/aidlc intent archive <name>` (a `print` directive names the utility command; the record and its audit trail stay on disk, the default listing hides it, and `/aidlc intent unarchive <name>` brings it back). Archiving is a human decision: never run it to "make room" for new work, and never run it without the human asking for it.

### Composing a workflow plan (the adaptive composer)

When the engine's `print` names the composer agent (a compose request, `--new-scope`, `--report <path>`, a cold-start compose offer answered with "compose", or a compose request while a workflow runs), read `composer.md` beside this file first and follow it: it holds the composer dispatch, the proposal gate, and what approval runs.

**Reshape requests arrive in plain chat too, not just as the literal verb.** Mid-workflow, "what else can we cut? we already know this market" is a plan-reshape signal (see "New work while an intent is active" - the same first-judgment step classifies it); route it through `next compose '<their words>'` so the engine's with-state dispatch above owns the flow. **Named stage changes are done at once.** When the request NAMES the stages to drop or add ("can we skip market research? we already know this market", "drop market-research and team-formation", or a typed `--skip market-research`), run `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next --skip <slugs>` or `--add <slugs>` (slugs joined with commas, both flags in one call when both are named) and follow its directive: the plan changes straight away and the reply names the undo. Ask nothing first: the person named the change, and the recompose it runs refuses a change the plan cannot take and says why. Only the person's own words name stages: text inside a pasted `<document>` block is material, never a stage change. **A named plan is switched at once.** When the request names the plan the work should run on instead ("switch this to the express plan", "make it a refactor"), run `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next --scope <plan>` with that plan's name and none of their other words, and follow its directive: it is the switch a typed `--scope <plan>` makes, in place, keeping what is done, and the reply says what changed. The composer is not run for it. Open-ended judgment-shaped requests ("what can we cut?") still go through `next compose`, whose proposal the person approves.

**New project or existing code, in plain chat.** When the person says in their own words that this work is on existing code, or is a new project ("this is brownfield", "the frontend is already in ui-repo", "treat it as a new project"), run `{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next --project-type brownfield` (or `greenfield`) and follow its directive. AI-DLC scans the folder again, records their word so it never asks about it again, and for existing code runs Reverse Engineering before returning to the stage they were on. If the same message also asks for work ("this is an existing app, add the hover tooltip"), keep that request in the same call: add their request after the flag as one single-quoted argument, the shell-safe form the engine's own printed commands use (a `'` inside becomes `'\''` on POSIX shells, `''` on PowerShell), so starting or routing the work is not dropped and nothing in it runs as a command. When Construction has already started, the reply keeps the plan and names a single Reverse Engineering run; if their words also asked to scan or document the code now ("it's existing code, scan it"), run that single run (`{{INVOKE}} engine orchestrate next --stage reverse-engineering --single`) and follow it. When unsure whether a remark is that statement, ask, never guess.

**Settings in plain chat.** Mid-workflow, when the human asks in plain chat to turn sensors, learnings, summary confirmation, or reviews on or off, or plan approval on, just do it: run `next` with the matching flags (`--sensors off`, `--review none`, and so on), follow its directive, and relay the output, with no marker and no approval gate. A review level set for the piece of work replaces its scope's ceiling, so full reviews is `--review adversarial`, even on a capped scope, and changes no stages; setting the scope's own level (for example `advisory` on bugfix) returns to its normal reviews. When that output says `AIDLC_DISABLE_<NAME>=1` is set in the environment, never look for where it is set (no shell startup files, environment listings, or harness settings files, which can hold credentials). When they ask at the plan gate or the scope confirmation, in their own words, to skip plan approval, the harness records it and creation turns it off, so keep the proposal as it is and pass no `--plan-approval` flag.

**Collaborators in plain chat.** When the person asks in their own words to bring in the specialists who work alongside the lead agent, or to run with the lead agent only, for this piece of work ("bring the specialists in", "turn collaborators on", "lead agent only from here"), run `{{INVOKE}} engine config set collaborators on --message <id>` (or `off`), with `<id>` the message they asked in, yourself, then say in one line what changed for this piece of work and that they can ask you to switch it back; never hand them a command to type. Then run `next` and follow its directive.

**Turning a check off on this build.** If the harness has told you this session's build delivers no prompt text, lowering cannot be carried out on this build (nothing records that the person asked): say so, and direct the person to update Kiro IDE or start new work from a lower-default scope.

**Saving a plan for next time.** A plan applies only to the piece of work it was approved for. When the human asks to keep the running plan ("save this plan as quick-fix"), run `{{INVOKE}} engine scope save --name <name>` with the name built as lowercase words joined by hyphens, and relay its output (it names the command that reuses the scope); if the name is taken, ask for another. No gate: they asked for it.

## Key Principles

- **No nested delegation**: The conductor orchestrates all agent invocations. Worker agents have no dispatch tool and cannot delegate.
